My research interests primarily revolve around the relationship between speech perception and production. Currently, I am investigating the influence of language-specific prosody on the emergence of speech rhythm, which plays a crucial role in constraining motor control during speech production. Additionally, I am interested in exploring cognitive aspects of speech processing in a broader context.

I finished my PhD at Phonetics and Phonology Laboratory under the supervision of Cécile Fougeron and Leonardo Lancia in 2023. My PhD dissertation focuses on how speech rhythm adapts to different speech production situations, i.e., those where speakers' auditory feedback is altered. I have also investigated how alterations in auditory feedback can influence speakers' production, particularly in terms of speech rhythm organization. This effect may be related to a change in the speakers' sense of agency concerning their voice.
